A 19-year-old woman who was on a night out with friends has died after a drive-by shooting that left three other people injured.

Vana-Reese Schuster-Togiatau, from Jimboomba, was hit in the head and neck when masked gunmen opened fire into a crowd outside Fitzy’s Loganholme, a pub in Brisbane’s south, on Saturday night.

She died in the Princess Alexandra hospital on Thursday morning.

Three men, aged 27, 37 and 38, were also hospitalised. One is recovering from surgery while the other two have been released.

Police are searching for those responsible for the attack, which happened on a night when the venue was hosting a boxing event.

Det Insp Ian Galpin was visibly emotional as he announced the young woman’s death to the media.

He offered his condolences to Schuster-Togiatau’s family and friends, adding that about 80 police officers were involved in the murder investigation.

“We will not stop until these people are identified and taken into custody,” he said.

“I’d ask our community to reflect on the fact that a 19-year-old woman, in the prime of her life, was simply going out for a night out with friends and has had her life taken from her in the most cowardly way.

“There are people out there who know exactly who was involved in this offence and exactly why this occurred.

“I’m asking those people to be brave enough to come forward with that information.”

Det Insp Galpin said the young victim’s family was “not doing well” and had requested their privacy be respected.

“It’s an incredibly difficult time for them, I can’t imagine the hurt that they’re going through,” he said.

Malick Yalcouye opened the scoring for the visitors, only for Mateo Kovacic to equalise before the break.

Georginio Rutter restored the Seagulls' advantage in the second half and nearly extended their lead, striking the crossbar and the side-netting. However, substitute Jeremy Doku helped shift the momentum, and Vitor Reis turned a Rayan Cherki free-kick into his own net to force penalties.

Back-up goalkeeper Geronimo Rulli then stepped up to become the hero. Phil Foden, Enzo Fernandez, Doku, and Cherki all successfully converted for City, allowing Rulli's saves against Yasin Ayari and Lewis Dunk to seal the victory.

Addressing Doku's influence, he continued: "I'm not sure he changed the game, but for sure he made an impact. Reflecting on the spot-kicks, Hurzeler added: "We will never blame players who miss chances or miss penalties. It is a team sport and we stick together. I saw a team who tried hard and a team who gave everything, also out of possession."

Brighton will not have to wait long for an opportunity to respond, as the two sides clash again in the Premier League this Saturday. The Seagulls return to the Etihad seeking to convert their attacking threat into three points, while City must swiftly refocus on maintaining their strong league form.

Having navigated a tricky cup fixture with a heavily rotated squad, Maresca is expected to recall his rested stars for the weekend. The swift turnaround promises another intense tactical battle as both managers look to exploit the weaknesses exposed during Wednesday's encounter.

#264. Stats Rundown: 3 numbers to know from Mavericks’ 120-96 win over the Oklahoma City Thunder

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Dallas Mavericks (3-2) blew out the Oklahoma City Thunder (4-1), 120-96, on Wednesday night in Dallas. Cooper Flagg led a balanced Dallas attack with 26 points, while Ajay Mitchell led the Thunder and all scorers with 29 points.

It was a dominant showing for this rising Mavericks team. OKC entered the night undefeated but seriously undermanned — Jalen Williams is out for the season, Cason Wallace is out indefinitely, while Chet Holmgren and Alex Caruso were out with minor injuries. This wasn't a game the Mavericks had to win, but it was as good an opportunity as ever to take down a championship favorite.

Dallas did so by jumping on OKC from tipoff and never letting up. The Mavericks outscored the Thunder in all four quarters and led for 81 percent of this game. Outstanding win.

The Thunder are a dangerous shooting team with Shai Gilgeous-Alexander running the show, but missing three of their better 3-point shooters, the Mavericks took advantage. Dallas made 16-of-45 from distance, while the Thunder were just 5-of-32.

That massive shooting disparity basically decided the game. Dallas wasn't on fire so to speak from deep, but took a ton of threes and made just enough. It was a team effort, as no Maverick made more than three 3s, while six Mavericks made at least two 3s. Kyrie Irving and Cooper Flagg made three apiece in the win.

Just as important as the lack of makes for OKC were the attempts — Dallas took 13 more threes than OKC, and the Mavericks did a decent job of running the shooters OKC had left off the line. Some of this is luck, for sure — OKC's Jared McCain went an uncharacteristic 0-for-6 — but credit the Dallas defense for digging in and doing the work on rotations and closeouts.

It was a rocky start for Dusty May's offensive system in the season opener against the Rockets, but since then the Mavericks offense has been mostly humming, and Dallas was at it again with another outstanding passing night. Dallas had 30 assists on 46 made buckets, with only nine turnovers.

May encourages ball movement and player movement, and at first it almost felt like the Mavericks were oversharing to start the season, getting a bit too far away from their best players. That balance has come back, led by Flagg's brilliance as a decision-maker. He had eight assists and zero turnovers against the Thunder as he continues to grow as a playmaker.

Caleb Martin hasn't had a good run in Dallas since being traded to the Mavericks in February of 2025, plagued by injuries and anemic shooting. Martin had a history of being a solid vet before coming to Dallas, so it was unfortunate just how much the wheels fell off once he landed in Dallas. That's why we're taking note of Martin's stellar first game of the season — 10 points 14 minutes off the bench, going 2-of-4 from beyond the arc.

Martin only scored in double-figures seven times last season, so for him to return from injury and immediately score 10 feels notable. If Martin can give this Mavericks team anything, it'll be a tremendous boost to what is suddenly looking like a very deep rotation.

#349. Yemen: Houthis Depriving Taizz Civilians of Food, Medicine

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image The Hayjat al-Abd road, which connects Aden to Taizz city, Yemen, September 26, 2026. © 2026 Photo by AFP via Getty Images

(Beirut) – A long-running humanitarian crisis in Taizz, Yemen’s third-largest city, has drastically worsened since October 2026, when the Houthi armed group forces fully encircled the city and blocked and attacked the remaining major supply route, Human Rights Watch said today. Since then, there have been severe shortages of fuel, food, water, medicine, cooking gas, and access by humanitarian agencies.

Houthi forces have surrounded Taizz since 2015, early in the conflict with the Yemeni government. While government-backed forces control the city center, Houthi forces have blocked access to all major roads connecting Taizz to the rest of the country, with the road connecting Taizz with Aden the most important. While international law does not prohibit sieges of military forces, the Houthis’ siege has for 12 years severely restricted civilian movement and impeded essential goods to the city’s residents.

“Renewed efforts by the Houthis to cut off food, water, and medicine to Taizz show their utter disregard for civilian lives,” said Niku Jafarnia, Yemen and Bahrain researcher at Human Rights Watch. “Throughout the 12-year-long siege of Taizz, the Houthis have bombed, starved, and denied humanitarian aid to a million residents of the city with scant adherence to international law.” 

The Houthis should immediately open vital roads for essential goods and aid to civilians in Taizz. All warring parties need to facilitate aid and the safe movement of civilians.

Human Rights Watch spoke to fifteen people in October, including five city residents, four doctors and six civil society workers. A Houthi spokesperson told Human Rights Watch on October 21 that Yemeni government forces, not Houthi forces, were blocking roads into the city. But residents, civil society workers, and United Nations officials placed responsibility for the shortages on the Houthis.

One resident told Human Rights Watch early in the escalation in fighting that vegetables were already “in very short supply” and that their prices had doubled. Another said on October 15 that he had been searching for wheat and flour for three days without finding any.

On October 14, the UN World Food Programme (WFP) said that they had not been able to access the “more than 600,000 people in Taiz who depend on humanitarian assistance,” which was “especially alarming” since most of Taizz’s districts are considered to have emergency levels of hunger. The WFP also said that “wheat flour stocks are nearly exhausted” in the city and that “remaining food supplies in local markets may last only a short time if access constraints continue.” 

On October 23, Jens Laerke, spokesperson for the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) stated that it had been “20 days and counting since Taiz has been cut off.”

The humanitarian crisis in Taizz appears to have been primarily caused by the Houthis cutting off the city from the rest of Yemen, which affects not only deliveries of food, but also water, fuel, and medicine, Human Rights Watch said. “The crisis in Taizz isn’t in the food itself, it’s in the fuel,” said the leader of a local civil society group. “Transporting food depends on fuel… and truck drivers’ costs have doubled.”

Residents described the difficulty in obtaining food and cooking gas since the siege tightened. One resident, who runs an organization that provides aid, said that the price of cooking gas had increased by about 400 percent. Others said that bottles of cooking gas had become hard to find.

Shortages of fuel have contributed to shortages of water. Akhil Iyer, the country representative in Yemen for the UN Children’s Fund (UNICEF) said that “diesel shortages threaten to bring water pumps to a halt, while the cost of trucked water has tripled since this latest escalation.”

Laurent Bukera, the UN humanitarian coordinator for Yemen, said in an October 20 statement that water production had been halved in Taizz city and that alternatives were “running out.”

The Houthis have restricted access to water for Taizz residents throughout the long siege, which Human Rights Watch previously found violated residents’ right to water. The Houthis have also been blocking water trucks: an organization tracking the siege said that water tank operators told them that they had been stopped at Houthi checkpoints when trying to bring water into the city.

The Houthi siege has severely affected health care for civilians in Taizz, Human Rights Watch said. Doctors and other health workers reported that road closures and fuel shortages prevented many civilians needing health care—especially those with cancer, kidney disease, hypertension, and other chronic diseases living outside the city center—from reaching hospitals and clinics.

One doctor said that patients living in government-controlled areas where there were no road closures still struggled to reach healthcare facilities in Taizz “because transportation is difficult and fuel, including gasoline, is unavailable or unaffordable.”

Health workers described shortages of medicine and other healthcare items. A doctor in Taizz said that she had seen many patients, including women and children, coming in with conflict-related injuries, but that there was a shortage of anesthesia for surgeries. International humanitarian law applicable to the armed conflict in Yemen does not prohibit siege warfare so long as the purpose is to achieve a military objective and not to starve or cause disproportionate harm to the civilian population. Starvation can include the intentional deprivation of medicine and other necessities, in addition to food and water. 

All warring parties, including a besieging army, have an obligation to facilitate access of humanitarian aid to civilians and not arbitrarily delay it. Parties must also ensure the freedom of movement of humanitarian workers, which can only be restricted temporarily for reasons of imperative military necessity.

Customary international humanitarian law prohibits as a war crime the use of starvation of civilians as a method of warfare by depriving them of objects indispensable to their survival, including by impeding relief supplies. Criminal intent can be inferred from the totality of the circumstances of the military campaign and does not require that people in fact starved.
