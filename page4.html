#40. Carlos Tevez reveals how a broken promise from Sir Alex Ferguson led to his infamous switch from Man United to Man City

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Published: 22:30 GMT, 28 October 2026 | Updated: 07:59 GMT, 29 October 2026

Former Manchester United forward Carlos Tevez has claimed Sir Alex Ferguson broke a promise to him before he left the club to join arch rivals Manchester City.

Tevez infamously made the switch from United, where he was on loan, to City in 2009. He would go on to win the Premier League for a third time, and hold a banner reading 'RIP Fergie' during the 2012 title celebrations.

The former forward would later try and explain his actions, but has now revealed that Ferguson is responsible for the reason why he left the club.

Tevez was appearing on the Rio Ferdinand Presents channel, and said: 'They spoke to me (before) the two year loan. After, if you play well, (we) pay the contract after one year.

'Fergie told me, “Carlos, now I'm going to pay out your contract and you're going to stay in Manchester". The team went to a pre-season. I remember this conversation with Fergie. He said “I’m going to pay for Berbatov but you, don't worry, I'm going to buy you and we'll do your contract.'

That didn't transpire, though, and United refused to pay the fee that would have his deal made permanent. He stayed at the club for another year before leaving.

Carlos Tevez (left) has suggested a broken promise from Sir Alex Ferguson (right) led to his Manchester United departure

Tevez would leave United for arch rivals Manchester City in 2009 and win another league title

'For me, the people understand,' Tevez said of the United fans, who have been split on their view of him since he departed. 'They don’t understand about Fergie speaking to me, or behind the scenes. What happened between Fergie and me, I think people don't understand that.'

He added: 'I think that Ferguson had, from the day we talked, up to six months to keep his word. And he didn't. On the contrary, he started leaving me on the bench. In my head, he started pushing me aside so he could bring down what I was worth. And that's what hurt me the most.'

Ferdinand was sympathetic towards Tevez, suggesting that his exit 'killed' United, who still haven't won a Premier League title since 2013.

And it seems Tevez, who is now a manager and was most recently in charge of Talleres in his native Argentina, still has a soft spot for the Red Devils, despite all that went on after his departure.

'For me it was hard Rio,' he added. 'It was hard because I felt like I'd given my heart to United. And I didn't feel the same.

'I didn't feel the same because, if you remember, we won the Champions League. We won the league and, and then, to bring down my price, they brought in a player and I carried on the same way for the first months. The second months, let's say, of the second year. But by the second, the second month, I didn't want to know any more.'

He added: 'I had a great time at United. The other team, City, for me, was like a revenge. I didn't get back what I gave.'

#111. Carlos Tevez reveals Sir Alex Ferguson role in his controversial Manchester United exit

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Carlos Tevez has accused Sir Alex Ferguson of breaking a promise over his Manchester United future, claiming the legendary manager began pushing him out of the team before his controversial move to Manchester City.

Tevez was appearing on the Rio Ferdinand Presents channel, during which he reflected on the breakdown of his relationship with Ferguson after helping the club win the Premier League and Champions League.

The Argentine initially joined United on a two-year loan deal after impressing at West Ham. Tevez recalled a conversation with Ferguson during the club’s pre-season tour of Los Angeles, in which the Scot assured him that his future at Old Trafford would be secured.

“Fergie told me, ‘Carlos, now I’m going to pay out your contract and you're going to stay in Manchester’,” Tevez explained. He claimed Ferguson had also reassured him that the club would complete a permanent deal, despite signing Dimitar Berbatov.

However, Tevez believes those assurances were not honoured, leaving him feeling unwanted after contributing to United’s success. The forward said the club’s decision to bring in another player appeared to be an attempt to reduce his value, while his own role in the team began to diminish.

“For me it was hard, Rio. It was hard because I felt like I'd given my heart to United. And I didn't feel the same,” Tevez said.

He added that Ferguson had six months to keep his word following their conversation in Los Angeles, but claimed the manager instead began leaving him on the bench and pushing him aside.

“He started pushing me aside so he could bring down what I was worth. And that's what hurt me the most,” Tevez said.

Ferdinand says the Argentine’s exit had a big impact on the dressing room.

“It didn't end well. I was disappointed. It killed us, man. Serious,” Ferdinand said.

#28. Rulli’s heroics earn Manchester City shootout success against Brighton

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

After Jérémy Doku’s second-half substitute appearance transformed Manchester City into a high-octane proposition, the smart money was on the momentum continuing into a victorious penalty shootout.

So it proved as Enzo Maresca’s men triumphed 4-3, Gerónimo Rulli the hero: his save from Lewis Dunk’s fifth spot-kick was decisive, the Argentinean also having repelled Yasin Ayari’s fourth effort.

While Jason Steele kept out Mateo Kovacic’s attempt, City progressed after Brighton twice took the lead – via Malick Yalcouyé and Georginio Rutter – before being pegged back by Kovacic and Vitor Reis.

Maresca was left beaming: “Since I’m here we never practised penalties,” said the City manager. “It always happens that the one that scored in the 90 minutes [Kovacic] misses the penalty. I am very happy.”

Of Doku, he said: “He made an impact. This is the reason they are there. Jérémy and the rest – to try to do their best, if they start from the beginning or if they come in from the bench try to make an impact.”

The tie might have gone either way, so the lottery of penalties felt apt to decide who went through before Saturday’s rematch back here in the league. City will now play League One Bradford in the quarter-finals.

“I watched all [Brighton’s] Premier League games and since the first day they always go front foot, man to man, so they’re going to be exactly the same on Saturday,” Maresca said.

His opposite number spoke of vengeance. “We lost in a shootout. We had enough chances to win the game – they had the luck today. We have to reset. We have a great chance on Saturday to take revenge,” said Fabian Hürzeler.

Brighton will stay up in the area. “Not sleeping in your own bed is not an advantage,” said the manager. “But we’ll go there with full energy and ready to go on Saturday.”

When Yalcouyé opened the scoring there was the proverbial hint of offside about the forward as his shot pinged off Rulli and in.

With no video assistant referee in the Carabao Cup the goal stood, though it was soon cancelled out by Kovacic: Phil Foden fed him and, from the edge of the penalty area, he threaded the ball beyond Steele low into the left corner.

For the second half Maresca introduced Rayan Aït-Nouri and Ryan McAidoo for Ayyoub Bouaddi and Floyd Samba. Instantly, though, City conceded: Brighton slipped through the lines with ease, and Jaouen Hadjam’s cross was slid in by Rutter.

At least there was pace in this foray, which those in blue sometimes lacked. But, also, Maresca’s team had become sloppy – a long-range Rayan Cherki pass that ended in Steele’s gloves was emblematic.

Olivier Boscagli’s corner that dipped viciously close to Rulli’s goalline was far more dangerous, so just after the hour Maresca introduced a first big gun – Doku, for Allan – this coming after Rutter ran in and went close to a second, underlining Brighton’s ascendancy.

At this point, though, the decision to allow his star quintet the night off was backfiring for Maresca. City lacked control and were toothless. Doku, again, cut through Brighton but there were no runners to prosper from his delivery infield.

Suddenly, those in green were having to answer questions. Foden’s burst that drew a free-kick from Rutter near the 18-yard line presaged one they could not answer. Cherki blasted at goal, Reis stuck out a boot, Steele was wrongfooted, and it was 2-2. Doku had lit up City, his presence terrorising Brighton’s defence and turning the tie.

“They are in a brilliant moment, scoring goals, many goals every game and winning, so we knew it was a tough game in terms of defending in the right way,” Maresca said. “We could have probably done something better defensively, but we started just three months ago [together]. #57. Man City keep Carabao Cup defence on track after Geronimo Rulli penalty heroics against Brighton in breathless tie - but Enzo Maresca faces toughest test yet when Seagulls return, writes JACK GAUGHAN

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Published: 21:52 GMT, 28 October 2026 | Updated: 23:00 GMT, 28 October 2026

Another of these in under 72 hours will do just fine. High stakes, a relentless pace. Enzo Maresca and Fabian Hurzeler produce pure chaos – and that is just on the touchline.

Somebody should stick GPS trackers on these two. Both managers hopped and jumped, right on the edge, right on the cusp of glory and disaster in equal measure. The intricacies of a match at both this quality but also this speed can do that.

A breathless tie and these were the second strings, remember. Separated only by penalties, only by Lewis Dunk’s effort being pushed away by stand-in goalkeeper Geronimo Rulli. City nudged into the quarter-finals via a shootout, where League One Bradford City now await.

More importantly, Brighton are back here on Saturday afternoon and will do so with a reasonable expectation of winning too. Undoubtedly, it represents Maresca’s biggest test of his short reign to make sure one league defeat doesn’t quickly become two.

He will be armed with far stronger artillery. Erling Haaland has been given the first half of this weekend off as annual leave alongside Elliot Anderson, Ruben Dias, Josko Gvardiol and Gianluigi Donnarumma – decisions made with mental rest in mind.

City built a head of steam as this fascinating spectacle wore on but will be able to do so boasting the division’s finest striker and a central midfielder who drives them on when the serious stuff occurs. Hurzeler, engagingly brilliant in that slightly manic fashion of his, will be hatching plans to answer those questions.

The 33-year-old can draw those up not far from here. Brighton are spending the rest of this week in Manchester and they were beginning to look at home, picking holes in the City system – one largely focused on going man-to-man, something that prompted their opener, Georginio rutter dropping off the front and finding Malick Yalcouye.

That Brighton didn’t kick on and finish this was a source of agitation for their boss. ‘The goals we conceded were very unlucky,’ he said. ‘They had the luck today. We have to reset. We have a great chance on Saturday to take revenge.’ He did add that sleeping in a hotel, ‘not in our beds’ for three nights is not exactly ideal, although their facilities in the area will be top.

Hurzeler wasn’t the only one wearing a look of frustration on the night, Maresca pent up when City first equalised through Mateo Kovacic. Andrew Madley had waved away appeals for a penalty, when Jaouen Hadjam’s hand halted City’s progress, and part of Maresca’s celebration once Kovacic swept in from the edge of the box was a small row with the fourth official.

That there had been a combined 16 changes between the teams from their weekend defeats and that they still manufactured this fraught night indicated that the match up in a few days should be mouth-watering. A shame, really, that the UK television audience won’t be able to watch the three o’clock kick off.

More of the same would go down a treat and you just cannot call it beforehand – especially when Hurzeler’s team play like this. ‘No doubt it will be the same on Saturday,’ Maresca smiled. ‘They are scoring many, many goals every game. They’re always on the front foot. It’s going to be exactly the same.’

Bring that on. Poetry, Brighton’s second. Within two minutes of the restart, pinning City into uncomfortable areas with a collection of first-time knocks, passing lines painting triangles up the left, and Hadjam rampaging forward to square for Rutter, whose thump into the far corner was impressive in both precision and power given the lack of back lift.

Yankuba Minteh later nearly snatched it for the visitors and Mats Wieffer struck a post.

#421. This map shows you which NYC restaurants are owned by private equity

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

For many consumers, “private equity” has become a four-letter word. Private equity firms have acquired the unpopular reputation of purchasing food establishments that then notoriously decline in quality, as new owners cut costs in search of new avenues to profit. They’ve also been criticized for displacing mom-and-pop shops in the process.

But beyond noticing the stereotypical signs of private equity backing—sudden spikes in new franchise locations and sleek, minimalist branding, to name two—consumers are often left guessing at a business’s ownership. That’s especially true in metropolises like New York City, where grabbing a bite means choosing from a whopping 31,000-plus food establishments. 

That’s where a trending new website steps in. On isitownedbyprivateequity.com, users can find the answers to the site’s titular question. Via an interactive map of New York City, you can zoom in on any bar, restaurant, or café and learn whether it’s owned or backed by private equity or venture capital, and whether it’s public or privately owned.

How the site works

The site was created by Dave Liebowitz, known on social media as DeFi Dave, who got the idea from a common complaint he’d seen on the internet.

“What initially inspired me to create it was walking around Manhattan on the weekends and seeing all the lines for different restaurants,” Liebowitz tells Fast Company via social media. “Then I would go online and see the discourse around private equity blaming them for the lines and decrease in quality.”

He went on to create the site with Grok Bot and Cursor in a single day (an experience he described as “psychedelic” in a follow-up post), then shared it on social media. Liebowitz’s X post announcing the site has since garnered more than 178,000 views.

So far, the site has amassed info on more than 5,000 NYC eateries. And it’s growing fast, with 1,320 of those listings being added in the day since the site’s launch.

Of the currently tracked restaurants, bars, and cafés, 28% are owned or backed by private equity, with firm Roark Capital alone accounting for 842 of them. (Nearly all of those are Dunkin’ Donuts and Subway locations—Roark acquired Dunkin’ in 2020 and Subway in 2024.)

Clicking on a business opens a window with tons of info, including its ownership status, nearby privately owned alternatives, and a scorecard for its neighborhood showing what share of food spots in that area are owned or backed by private equity. According to the site, that data all comes from investor and parent-company portfolio pages, as well as from Wikipedia articles that cite primary sources.

The story beyond private equity

Liebowitz emphasizes that being a private or family-owned business doesn’t automatically correlate with quality.

In another post on X, he took aim at Black Seed Bagels—a chain with sleek, clean branding that fits right in with the private equity crowd, but is actually privately owned. According to Liebowitz, that didn’t stop the bagel shop from skimping on the lox on his sandwich, much to his chagrin.

His map accounts for stereotype-defying establishments with its “before vs. after the deal” window. For private equity-owned or -backed businesses with enough data, the site shows how their quality has changed over time, based on their American Customer Satisfaction Index (ACSI) scores.

For example, Panera Bread has dropped by 4 points on the ACSI since its 2017 acquisition by JAB Holding Co., while Subway gained 5 points since being bought by Roark.

Rather than passing hard judgment on “good” or “bad” places to eat, Liebowitz says he hopes his site can simply help consumers be better informed about where their money is going.

“I believe more transparency is always good, especially when it comes to where people decide to spend their money and what businesses to support,” he says. “In addition, I hope people use the site to be more objective about restaurants they patronize.”

Looking ahead, Liebowitz plans to expand the site to include San Francisco and Los Angeles establishments, as well as to let users submit their own information about businesses to build on the site’s existing data. He also hopes to create maps for industries other than food service that are often targeted by private equity firms, including nursing homes, real estate, healthcare, and dentistry.
