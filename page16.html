#369. Inside March Of Dimes' The Collective Event

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Scary Mommy and Romper were media sponsors for The Collective’s “It Starts With Mom” campaign, which hosted a New York City event last week.

Last week, I had the good fortune to attend an event from The Collective called “It Starts With Mom,” for which both Scary Mommy and Romper were media sponsors. The Collective is the brainchild of March of Dimes, one of this country’s most well-known nonprofits fighting for the wellbeing of moms and babies across the United States.

The Collective kicked off its second annual, bi-coastal event at New York City’s Chelsea Piers, where guests were first treated to delicious appetizers while they shopped at a Kendra Scott pop-up, wrote letters to NICU moms, got live drawings of themselves, and more. Later, guests were seated to a lunch and learn, during which NBC4 anchor Natalie Pasquerella opened the event with an honest tale of her own experience as a NICU mom, followed by a frank, moving onstage chat with March of Dimes CEO Cindy Rahman.

During their chat, Rahman candidly spoke of her own postpartum experience with her son.

Then, Dr. Michael Warren, March of Dimes’ Chief Medical and Health Officer, moderated a panel in which they discussed the necessity for improved postpartum mental health care for moms, the need for a village (especially when villages can be hard to come by) and how women need to be their own best advocates, especially while pregnant. The panel included NICU mom Danielle Olivera; women’s health expert, author and TV and radio personality Dr. Jennifer Wider; Dr. Nastassia K. Harris, RNC-MNN, DNP, IBCLC, who shared her tales of advocating for Black women throughout pregnancy; and Elizabeth Baron, a licensed mental health counselor and psychotherapist.

As Rahman noted in an earlier press release, “Listening to moms, understanding their experiences, and ensuring their voices are heard are foundational to addressing the challenges we face.The Collective is about creating a space where moms feel supported and empowered, and inspiring all of us to continue to show up for one another in big and small ways. Through this effort, we are establishing a culture where every mom feels she can seek the care she needs, and where families, communities, and organizations come together to ensure no mom has to navigate motherhood alone.”

The event was a moving example of what can happen with women — and, yes, some men were there! — come together and have an open dialogue about some of the road blocks facing pregnant and postpartum women in this country.

Below, guests affix color-coded flowers to a board in honor of different maternal struggles.

It was an empowering, emotional day, and I’m so glad Scary Mommy and Romper got to be a part of it.

#285. Woim is the earth-guzzling, self-bisecting sokoban pick-me-up I needed after a week of games industry disasters

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

While ladling city-builders into the Maw's feeding trough this week, I remarked that being a worm must really suck. Then came the closure of BioWare, layoffs across other EA studios, further depressing genAI revelations, allegations of antisemitism at indie gaming events, a big Capcom data breach, and yet more worrying tidyings about Arkane.

I rescind my earlier observation: I would like to be a worm. A happy little worm, tunnelling after golden apples in the moist and forgiving earth, where there are no emails or press releases at all. The only thing I have to worry about down here is my butt growing so big I get stuck behind it. And also getting chopped in half by sliding doors, but it turns out this may actually help with the first problem. See for yourself - video below.

Ah, to be a soil-dwelling invertebrate in Woim, a puzzle game with a touch of the sokobans, consisting of 50 levels and an unspecified quantity of secrets, scattered across four subterranean worlds. The idea in each level is to crawl around a grid layout to reach a golden apple, opening the exit.

As with non-videogame worms, this involves a certain amount of ingesting earth and pooping it out behind you, but you can't just devour the whole layout. You'll need to guzzle red apples that make you longer, so that you can (for example) keep pressing a switch with your body as you slither towards a door. Or you might have to shove a rock onto that switch. Or shove that rock onto a water jet to propel it somewhere you can't reach.

Later on, there are breakable eggs that must be carried to nests, daisies that push back outward when you take your weight off them, scissors with which to snip yourself into separately functioning halves, and hats. I don't think this is an entirely accurate worm simulator. As in sokoban, the major problem is trapping yourself, but you can undo moves without penalty by tapping Z.

I've played six levels, and it's a jolly production. The music wobbles and boobles along, and your worm has a habit of looking at you expectantly when you get stuck that makes me giggle. Read more about it on Steam. If, unlike me, you've had a really uplifting week and are in sore need of a sokobanlike charged with maximum ontological dread, may I recommend Void Stranger.

#407. Adrian Smith + Gordon Gill Architecture designs "fluid" skyscraper Lilli for Miami waterfront

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

US studio Adrian Smith + Gordon Gill Architecture has unveiled its latest skyscraper for Miami, designed to display “quiet refinement” on a slender site on the city’s waterfront.

Called Lilli, the residential skyscraper development is the latest in Miami from Adrian Smith + Gordon Gill Architecture (AS+GG). The studio is known for designing some of the world’s tallest buildings, including the world’s upcoming tallest skyscraper, the Jeddah Tower.

At 636 feet tall (193 metres), Lilli is planned for a narrow site in Miami’s Edgewater neighbourhood.

The luxury skyscraper will feature rounded edges and will have terraces on the water-facing side, with larger patios running up the opposite face.

Its design is characterised by rounded edges and large spans of glass interrupted by metallic floor plates.

“Our vision is for Lilli’s interior design to feel inseparable from its architecture,” said AS+GG design partner Juan Betancur.

“The fluidity of the building carries through the interior spaces, while a restrained palette of natural materials allows light, landscape, and views of Biscayne Bay to become part of the experience.”

“The result is a sense of quiet refinement that feels distinctly connected to its waterfront setting.”

The skyscraper will house 117 residences of varying sizes, spread across its 53 storeys, as well as amenities including lounges, pools and spas.

The ground floor will have a lobby for residents with large metallic columns and minimalist detailing.

At the top of the skyscraper, a pool will sit on the edge of a terrace underneath a bulkhead with the same reflective metal treatment seen elsewhere in the exterior.

Lilli is the latest in a series of residential skyscrapers being designed by the studio, which recently completed a “yacht-inspired” building in the city.

Residential skyscraper development has proceeded at a steady pace in Miami, with Bugatti recently unveiling its first branded skyscraper in the city.

#81. Inside Norwich City's slump under Philippe Clement: How Belgian's 24/7 intensity is leaving players running on empty and staff mentally drained - with 'absentee landlord' Mark Attanasio adding to toxicity

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Published: 12:00 GMT, 23 October 2026 | Updated: 12:00 GMT, 23 October 2026

In the early days of Philippe Clement’s reign at Norwich City, the Belgian worked so late into the night at the club's training ground that when he returned to his hotel, he had to plead with the staff to reopen the kitchens and bung a pizza in the oven.

Clement, described by sources as an ‘all-consuming perfectionist’, was simply too busy transforming a club that had become so toxic supporters were setting off flares outside Carrow Road and lobbing tennis balls on to the pitch.

When he arrived almost a year ago, only Sheffield Wednesday, a club hit with a 12-point deduction, sat below Norwich in the Championship. The Canaries had lost all eight home games of the season and were facing their first relegation to League One in 15 years.

And yet the 6ft 3in Clement, through what club insiders called his ‘aura’ and relentless work ethic, hauled them from the doldrums and into the play-off hunt. From the start of January to the end of the season, only Southampton won more points than Norwich.

In the summer, American billionaire owner Mark Attanasio declared that all the talk inside the club was how to ‘get the team into the top two next year’.

Only, Norwich are nowhere near the top two. They are back in the doldrums. Their 1-0 defeat by Portsmouth last weekend was their fifth in a row in the Championship, their eighth in 11 league matches this season, and left them just a point outside the relegation zone.

Philippe Clement is feeling the heat at Norwich, with his side losing eight of their 11 league matches this season

The fanbase may not be hurling tennis balls in fury again just yet but as the Norwich players completed their customary lap of appreciation around Carrow Road on Saturday, the supporters sent them back down the tunnel with chants of ‘You’re not fit to wear the shirt’ ringing in their ears.

Norwich are shipping goals at a faster rate than even under predecessor Liam Manning this season but, in trying to shore up the defence in recent weeks, it has come at the expense of goals for Clement’s side.

The Belgian's magic appears to have worn off. That round-the-clock drive, something he expects of everyone else at the club too, has stopped reaping its rewards.

Clement gave his charges one day off last December and it wasn’t Christmas Day. A week after the season ended, he had his players return for a 10-day boot camp. While other clubs gave their squad time off during the recent three-week international break, Clement’s men had just the odd day here and there.

Clement joked himself that the only time he wasn’t spending every waking second thinking about fixing Norwich’s plight, was the two hours he was hitting golf balls with his wife at the local driving range.

During the break, Clement held a meeting with the entire football department at their Colney training ground – from the data analysts, to the technical staff, to sporting director Ben Knapper – that lasted for nearly seven hours as they tried to figure out what was going wrong. He is known for not being shy in holding frank exchanges of views with his staff.

That intensity – and the demand for everyone else to buy into it – is fine and dandy when you’re winning matches. It inspires everyone around you, sweeps them up, and carries them with you. It soon starts to drag when you’re not winning anymore.

With no time for rest and recuperation, Norwich are running on empty and anyone who has watched their recent performances can see it.

Norwich players are running on empty and anyone who watches them can see it

Sporting director Ben Knapper faced chants calling for his dismissal towards the end of Norwich's loss to Portsmouth last weekend

It’s having an impact behind the scenes, too. Players and staff are said to be mentally fatigued, with little time to spend with their families. Some are struggling even to find time to get a haircut. One source close to the dressing room noted there’s been more colds going around the place because people are so run down.

What’s clear is that Clement is not for changing. ‘There's only one way, and that's to work hard, to stay positive, and to push your luck to the right side, and then it comes back,’ he said after the Portsmouth defeat. ‘I’ve always been a fighter, and I will fight really hard for this club to get everything back.’

‘They can put me in Siberia, it doesn't matter,’ he said during his time at Rangers. ‘I’m always working, I'm at it seven days a week.’

Upcoming games against Charlton, Watford and Derby, three sides also in the bottom third of the table, are crucial if Clement is to stop the toxic tide from crashing down once more.

Injuries have, again, hurt Norwich. At times last season, the Canaries were playing matches with as many as 14 senior players missing, with one fit striker and a defensive midfielder on the right wing.

It prompted the club to hire a firm to audit their practices, take advice from Liverpool and NBA teams and completely overhaul the medical department. A new head of physiotherapy and chief medical officer joined over the summer following the appointment of Dr Paul Ford from the British Olympic Association as the new performance director overseeing all elements of athlete management, including medical, performance, nutrition and psychology.

It’s not the number of injuries this time around so much as the importance of the players who are missing. Kenny McLean, the club’s longest-serving player, Scotland international, and inspirational captain, has missed the last three defeats. It meant that, against Portsmouth, Norwich had only two players over the age of 25 in their starting XI – and one of them was the goalkeeper. It’s a squad missing know-how and leadership.

Top scorer Mo Toure, who’s bagged 16 goals from his 21 matches since joining in January, is now out too after needing surgery on a shoulder injury while winger Ali Ahmed is likely to miss most of the season with a torn ACL.

The Canaries wanted an experienced winger in the summer only to spend £8million on 23-year-old Andre Brooks (left) from Sheffield United

While some fear Clement's time at Carrow Road is running out, the Belgian has credit in the bank for the miracles he worked last season

While there is naturally scrutiny starting to come Clement’s way, the general feeling is that the 52-year-old has enough credit in the bank for the miracles he worked last season. Much more of the anger, instead, is being directed at sporting director Knapper, who faced chants calling for his dismissal towards the end of the Portsmouth defeat.

Knapper, the former Arsenal loan manager, is on to his fourth permanent manager in three years after inheriting then sacking David Wagner before appointing Johannes Hoff Thorup and Manning.

When he first came to the club, Knapper talked about the need to lower the age of a squad which included the likes of Shane Duffy and Ashley Barnes, but the fear is Norwich have now gone too far the other way. Only Watford average a younger starting XI in the Championship this season. It was understood the Canaries wanted an experienced winger in the summer only to spend £8million on 23-year-old Andre Brooks from Sheffield United.

The frustration, more than anything, centres on who is holding the decision-makers to account and what is the plan for supporters to get behind. Norwich are into their fifth year in the Championship since relegation from the Premier League in 2021-22 and continue to drift.

Attanasio, the long-time owner of the Milwaukee Brewers who took over from Delia Smith and her late husband Michael Wynn-Jones, is rarely at matches. There are regular Zoom meetings but he leaves the everyday running of the football side to Knapper.

A host of Norwich legends returned to Carrow Road last Saturday to pay their respects to former owner and honorary life president Wynn-Jones, who died last month aged 85. All the board and ownership group were there except for Attanasio.

Across the Pond, he was celebrating the Brewers reaching the World Series for the first time in 44 years. The Brewers have enjoyed great success due to their use of data, punching above their weight significantly as one of the smaller teams in Major League Baseball, and Attanasio wants Norwich to do the same.

To make matters worse, his son, Mike, started blocking Norwich fans on X when they sent him their thoughts on the matter. ‘He’s put in more blocks than (defender Jose) Cordoba this season,’ remarked one supporter.

This did nothing to dispel the grumbles that Norwich are in the hands of an absentee landlord.

Owner Mark Attanasio claims his success at the Milwaukee Brewers is built on his four cornerstones for how to run a club - but Norwich aren't exhibiting them

One former member of staff remarked this week that after Delia sold up, there was a noticeable shift inside the building from the feeling of a family club to a more American business-minded approach.

Attanasio claims his success at the Brewers is built on his four cornerstones for how to run a club: fielding a perennially competitive team, delivering the best fan experience, remaining a leader in the community and being an organisation where people want to work.

Norwich began to charge for parking at Carrow Road this summer. They were forced to shelve plans for the stadium which would have involved 3,000 season-ticket holders moving to new seats. Redundancies towards the end of last season affected morale.

And they are, most certainly, not perennially competitive. They are, if anything, perennially treading water and find themselves doing so once again, no matter how tirelessly Clement wants everyone to keep kicking.

#363. Mexico: Delay in Legal Reform Harms People With Disabilities

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image A dog and a woman with a disability outside the Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation in Mexico City, Mexico, March 24, 2026. © 2026 Gerardo Vieyra/NurPhoto via AP Photo

(Mexico City) – Mexico’s Senate should amend legislation approved by the Chamber of Deputies on October 20, 2026, to ensure that people with disabilities can appoint supporters to make legal decisions by April 1, 2027, as originally planned, Human Rights Watch said today. The current legislation would postpone the deadline for implementing the National Code of Civil and Family Procedures, which requires these appointments, to July 1, 2031.

The code, adopted in June 2023, introduced reforms to improve access to justice and guarantee the right of people with disabilities throughout Mexico to make legally recognized decisions. However, implementation has lagged. Mexico City is the only jurisdiction to have declared the code partially in force, including provisions allowing adults to designate supporters to help them exercise their legal capacity.

“If the Senate fails to amend the legislation to allow the original deadline for appointing supporters to take effect, local judges will face a legal vacuum,” said Carlos Ríos Espinosa, associate disability rights director at Human Rights Watch. “While judges can no longer bar someone from making autonomous decisions, the procedure to help people make those decisions has not yet entered into force. This could leave people with disabilities at risk of losing their property or suffering harm to their health and physical integrity.”

The 2023 code abolished interdiction, a discriminatory guardianship procedure that allowed courts to deprive adults, particularly people with intellectual or psychosocial disabilities, of their right to make legally recognized decisions. Under this system, guardians could make decisions concerning a person’s property, finances, and personal affairs, often without respecting the person’s will and preferences.

Interdiction was abolished on June 8, 2023. Mexico’s Supreme Court has recognized that these procedures are incompatible with international human rights standards and that their repeal does not depend on the gradual implementation of the new code.

However, abolishing interdiction without providing effective alternatives can leave some people without essential legal protections.

The new code establishes an extraordinary judicial procedure for appointing supporters when a person’s will and preferences cannot be determined despite considerable efforts. This mechanism allows a judge to appoint someone to facilitate the exercise of legal capacity, subject to strict safeguards against abuse and requirements to ensure the best possible interpretation of the person’s will and preferences.

Without this procedure, people may face serious consequences. Consider, for example, someone who falls into a coma without having left any advance directives concerning their property, health care, or family affairs. If urgent decisions are required, the absence of a legal mechanism to appoint supporters could put their property at risk or jeopardize their health and physical integrity.

Unlike interdiction, the extraordinary appointment of supporters does not authorize courts to declare a person legally incapable or place them under a general guardianship regime that substitutes another person’s decisions for their own.

International human rights law recognizes that everyone has the right to make decisions about their own lives and requires governments to ensure that people with disabilities have access to the support they require to do so.

Organizations of people with disabilities, older people’s rights groups, professional associations, and human rights organizations have called for the extraordinary procedure for appointing supporters to be exempted from postponement.

The Senate still has an opportunity to amend the legislation approved by the Chamber of Deputies and to introduce an exception allowing this procedure to be implemented as originally planned.

“The government may reasonably require additional time and resources to implement a reform of this magnitude, but delaying a critical safeguard for people with disabilities for nearly five more years is unjustifiable,” Ríos Espinosa said. “The Senate still has an opportunity to correct this situation and prevent people from being exposed to abuse and serious violations of their rights.”
