FIRST ON FOX: Sen. Bernie Moreno (R-OH) is taking aim at the Democratic mayor of Columbus, Ohio, over a memorandum of understanding (MOU) that was signed with the African nation of Ghana promoting a joint nursing program.

"Instead of investing in Ohio’s domestic workforce, you and the Columbus City Council chose to direct $225,000 in public dollars towards recruiting nurses from overseas," Moreno wrote to Mayor Andrew Ginther on Wednesday morning, expressing "disappointment" over the MOU in Columbus, the state capital of Ohio.

"But this figure is only a fraction of the approximately $8.3 million in taxpayer dollars you have lit on fire to benefit noncitizens and foreign governments since you were elected as mayor."

Moreno’s criticism comes after the city and Ghanaian officials signed a memorandum of understanding in September establishing the Ghana-Columbus Nursing Partnership Programme. The agreement is intended to strengthen nursing education, expand workforce development and create legal pathways for qualified Ghanaian nurses to enter Ohio’s healthcare workforce.

#310. NYC office-to-apartment mega project resumes after near disaster

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Crews lifted sagging floors at the Midtown East Pfizer HQ conversion after a July structural failure tied to missing reinforcement. New York's conversion pipeline continues, supported by state law property tax incentives and new permits.  AI Summary

Crews have finished lifting the sagging floors at the former Pfizer headquarters, the largest office-to-apartment conversion in city history.

A July structural failure forced evacuations across seven Midtown East blocks and, for a fleeting moment, threw a menacing cloud over a nationwide push to turn empty offices into housing.

Two columns buckled at the 1,600-unit conversion, which touched off inspections citywide, and stop-work orders followed. Concern spread nationwide, generating questions over cost, safety and design in adaptive reuse of decades-old office buildings.

Cities from Washington to Los Angeles have bet that converting vacant offices can add housing faster than ground-up construction. New York leads that push, and others emulate its tax breaks and zoning changes.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani inherited a conversion pipeline of about 12,000 units and has embraced it as a pillar of his own housing affordability pitch. Mamdani has leaned into commercial-to-residential conversion as part of a multi-pronged approach to increasing housing supply.

More than 40 other conversions are underway or planned citywide, adding more than 14,000 homes. The city has issued 16 new building permits for conversions since July.

The pipeline is holding steady. State tax law’s 467-m program offers a property tax exemption of up to 35 years for rental conversions that include affordable units. A June 30 deadline this year locked in the tax break.

Nate Bliss, founder of Latent Urban Ventures, told HousingWire TBD that applications are picking back up.

“There’s a lot of activity of people trying to get their projects moving in the next six months, so they can meet the June 30, 2028, deadline” for a 30-year break for projects that start by mid-2028, Bliss said. “We still have a long way to go and plenty of office stock to convert.”

This past summer, two columns buckled on the 21st floor of the Pfizer building make-over, under floor area recently added to the top of the building. The project’s structural engineer blamed missing steel reinforcement, rather than design. Inspectors found no imminent structural dangers.

New York City Buildings Commissioner Ahmed Tigani said in a social media video that the tower “has remained stable” and poses no danger to the public. Developer engineers designed a plan to jack up floors above the affected area, which had dropped about six inches, Tigani said.

Senior department engineers and an independent third-party firm vetted the plan before approval.

“That plan has moved forward and is now complete,” Tigani said.

Some lower-floor work has been allowed to continue. Upper floors remain limited to housekeeping and repairs tied to the fix.

“We will not allow work to proceed unless we are confident that all could be done safely,” Tigani said.

MetroLoft and David Werner Real Estate Investments lead the project, which includes about 400 affordable, rent-stabilized apartments.

Inspections of 180 sites turned up 65 violations across 38 locations. They led to 18 partial stop-work orders and one full halt. Work has resumed at some of those sites.

Deals continue. In September, Capstone Equities and BH3 Management landed a $208 million loan through Northwind Group to convert upper floors of a three-year-old, never-occupied 24-story downtown Brooklyn tower into 239 apartments.

BH3’s Adam Falk told The Wall Street Journal the conversion should take 18 to 20 months instead of four to five years for new development.

The Gunners, in the midst of a five game ‘playoff’ for the league title with Manchester City, as well as a Champions League semi-final against Atletico Madrid on the horizon, raced into a three-goal lead by half-time at the Emirates, earning an uproarious reception as the players made for the tunnel at half-time.

The previous weekend had seen Arsenal labour to a nervy 1-0 win over Newcastle and a stodgy 1-1 away draw with Atletico Madrid. Bukayo Saka had been parachuted back into the team after an extended absence; but it was clear that Mikel Arteta was looking for ways to try to ease the ketchup out of the bottle, so to speak.

Martin Zubimendi, who, at that point had played more league minutes than any other outfield Arsenal player, dropped to the bench. Myles Lewis-Skelly started in midfield for the first time in his short Gunners career. Myles was supposed to start an FA Cup tie at home to Wigan in that position before Riccardo Calafiori was injured in the warm-up, meaning Lewis-Skelly reverted to left-back with Bukayo Saka playing in a central role instead.

The BBC Sport match report for the Fulham game said, ‘Boss Arteta has to be given credit for taking what could be perceived as risk by playing left-back Myles Lewis-Skelly in midfield for the first time and leaving out Martin Zubimendi, who has played the most minutes of any outfielder in the Gunners squad. The 19-year-old Lewis-Skelly looked assured in the role and, with Calafiori, Saka and Eberechi Eze all in the side, the Arsenal attack flowed better than it has done for many weeks.’

Myles kept his place for the second leg of the Champions League semi-final against Atleti and again for the crunch away win at West Ham. He then started the Champions League semi-final. With Declan Rice, Mikel Merino and Martin Zubimendi going to the final weekend of the tournament, as well as Odegaard, Eze and Bruno going to the latter stages, he had a clear run at the position.

The signing of Bruno Guimaraes, who could have offered a further road-block to Myles’ progression. There were also strong rumours that some people in the club (!) were touting Lewis-Skelly around in a bid to raise the funds for a much-needed attacking A-lister.

While this talk was swirling around in the summer, Myles’ response was to score in a pre-season friendly against Como. What followed was even more impressive, Bruno came into the season with some fitness concerns. Lewis-Skelly’s form was such that his presence in midfield made that a total non-issue. Overwhelmed by threats—and fearing for her personal safety Satya Rhodes-Conway the current mayor of Madison, Wisconsin in the US, announced on October 23 that she was dropping her bid for reelection. "The price of being in public office has gotten too high,” she said.

Globally, women in public life face alarming levels of political violence, including tech-facilitated sexual and gender-based violence, with little protection or recourse. While research finds chillingly high rates of violence against both women and men in politics, women are at greater risk and face more sexualized and gendered violence.

After Australian MP Helen Haines was deliberately hit by a car, doctors had to amputate both of her legs. In South Africa, a bloody campaign season included the killing of party activist Jeanette Magugu and a brutal home invasion against mayoral candidate Nhongo Solo. Experts increasingly warn that political violence deters women from running for office. With growing evidence that women’s leadership brings economic and social benefits, we all lose when women are pushed out of politics.
