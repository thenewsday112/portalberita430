#287. Cyberpunk 2077's new dual wielding has already been modded to include shotguns and rifles, provided you're down to get "janky and wildly overpowered"

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

It was only ever going to be a matter of time, but I'm still surprised just how quickly Cyberpunk 2077 modders have taken pistol dual wielding mechanic the RPG's Edgerunners 2 update has just added in, and opened it up to include any gun Night City has to offer. Yep, twinning shotguns, assault rifles, sniper rifles, or any combo of those is now possible. Though, you'll have to make peace with the fact enemies will struggle to match your firepower.

A number of different modders instantly decided to take on the task of extending the tag which governs Cyberpunk's new dual wielding perk to include everything with a trigger. There's Dual Wield Anything by long-time Cyberpunk modder E3roKK, Dual Wield Anything and Everything for 2.5 by Jollywankers, and Expanded Dual Wielding by JericoHellden. All three have been released within the last 24 hours.

JericoHellden's mod also includes the option to control each gun separately, rather than CD Projekt Red's default of automatically alternating between the two when firing. If you're only interested in twinning pistols, but do want the ability to independently fire each gun, another new mod - Independent Pistol Controls (Dual Wielding) by Dederara - might be for you.

Here are clips showing Jollywankers' (directly below) and Dederara's (the further down of the two) works in action:

It's worth noting that, in line with the reason CD Projekt Red gave for limiting it to pistols in their official update, dual wielding other guns without any measures in place to make enemies tougher does blunt the challenge of fighting your way through Night City. JericoHellden declared it a "janky and wildly overpowered" experience, and E3roKK made clear that anyone expecting animations not to get a bit clippy or awkward at times is barking up the wrong tree.

If you're hoping to dual wield katanas, unfortunately it looks like melee weapons will take a bit more time to figure out properly, with some of these mods including them only as a 'try it but don't expect things to work yet' afterthought to the ranged main course. Makes sense, given the official integration is designed for lead slingers rather than slappy cybersticks.

The latest version of Cyber Engine Tweaks, the framework mod needed for any Cybeprunk mod setup that's not limited to the likes of light visual tweaks, is a requirement for all for of the mods mentioned here. Redscript is also needed for Expanded Dual Wielding.

#359. Families and Students Need Guidance on AI

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Delaware families and policymakers are wrestling with the same issue troubling Americans nationwide: how to handle artificial intelligence.

In 2024, lawmakers in the Blue Hen State created a committee to study AI. Good thing, because parents and teachers badly need guidance on this issue.

Yet AI technology has advanced rapidly over the past two years, so the committee should prioritize drafting recommendations for K-12 schools.

To date, state officials have set only “voluntary” guardrails around AI use in elementary and secondary schools, according to the Delaware-based Caesar Rodney Institute. In May, legislators considered a proposal to create a pilot program that would allow 12 schools to use AI technology.

>>> What’s Really Behind the Call to Regulate AI

Still, the final report on the activities would not be due until 2029, and this year’s freshman class will be seniors by then. These students will be gone before lawmakers act on the report’s findings. Instead, lawmakers should focus on helping students in school today.

Besides, given the pace of change in AI technology, this proposal is too little, too late.

Fortunately, legislative models already exist that lawmakers can apply to allow the use of AI in schools, protect student data and prohibit nonacademic AI activities in classrooms.

Heritage Foundation research explains that lawmakers need not explicitly ban or allow the use of AI in schools. For school officials who allow AI in classrooms, however, educators should adopt training programs for teachers and students. These programs should help teachers and students draft prompts and questions for generative AI bots; create media using AI, such as audio, video and pictures; edit student writing; detect biases in AI results; evaluate different AI bots to decide which are the most effective; and protect student privacy.

Local school boards should adopt policies stating that students may use AI for schoolwork only when specifically allowed by teachers.

The U.S. Department of Education is urging state and local authorities to develop solutions. “Research and experience have demonstrated that technology is a valuable instructional tool when it supports strong pedagogy and effective teaching practices,” writes Kirsten Baesler, assistant secretary for elementary and secondary education. “The U.S. Department of Education believes these decisions [about education technology] are best made by states and local communities.”

Policymakers should move quickly. Even if self-reported student usage numbers are slightly inflated, the share of students using AI remains remarkable.

Education officials in large school systems such as New York City and Los Angeles are attempting to prohibit the use of AI for this school year. With so many students already using bots, however, a ban on AI will be difficult to enforce.

>>> Meta Abandons Its AI-Generating Tool, but Public-Safety Guardrails Are Needed

Instead of prohibiting AI, policymakers should draw on existing research to select appropriate educational applications of the technology. For example, lawmakers should block the use of companion bots that simulate personal interactions and develop relationships with users.

Instead, educators can give students access to generative bots that use data to summarize complex information. Teachers can benefit from AI programs that help organize lesson plans. When teachers assign students to use AI, students should report how they used the technology.

Parents should have the choice to opt out of these assignments and have students complete the work using other methods.

State policymakers must focus on boosting student outcomes as technology evolves. Federal officials agree. Ms. Baesler writes: “The question should not be whether teachers and students use technology. The question should be whether the technology we ask teachers and students to use improves learning and students’ academic outcomes.”

As Delaware’s AI committee drafts recommendations, officials should focus on student success. Those on both sides of the political aisle—and on either side of AI debates—can agree on this.

Then, parents and teachers can share best practices and offer solutions to help students across all 50 states.

#409. Liverpool's The Beatles museum to relocate to 1960s shopping centre

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Photography by Neil Martin via Unsplash

British multimedia company Apple Corps has announced that The Beatles Story museum in Liverpool will move from its site on the Royal Albert Dock to the St Johns Shopping Centre and former Radio City Tower.

More than quadrupling the footprint of the attraction’s current dockside site, the relocation will provide around 9,300 square metres of space for immersive exhibition design, original artefacts and reconstructed sets chronicling different eras of The Beatles as they rose to international fame.

The majority of The Beatles Story will be located in what was formerly St Johns Market in St Johns Shopping Centre, which was designed by English architect James A Roberts and built opposite Liverpool Lime Street station in 1969.

The Grade II-listed Radio City Tower, also known as St Johns Beacon, will be transformed into a Yellow Submarine-themed observation platform with views across the city.

Formerly a communications tower with a revolving restaurant, the transformed 138-metre-tall structure will operate as a standalone attraction complementing The Beatles Story museum.

“Liverpool was, and still is, music city, and now we are expanding The Beatles Story Experience in Liverpool – how far out,” said Ringo Starr. “The beat goes on.”

“If you’d told us this was going to happen when we were hanging out and getting started, we’d never have believed it,” added Paul McCartney. “The extraordinary plans the Apple Corps team have shown me for The Beatles Story take me right back to that journey, from Liverpool to the world and back home again.”

“It couldn’t have happened without Liverpool and I can’t wait for people to be able to step into our story and share the experience.”

Originally established on the Royal Albert Dock in 1990, The Beatles Story is set to relocate in 2028, aligning with the release of the upcoming The Beatles movie.

Apple Corps, which was founded by the four band members in 1968 to oversee The Beatles’ business interests, will take over ownership of the attraction from Mersey Ferrys and oversee the upcoming relocation.

“This is the kind of ambition I want to see more of across Britain – backing the things our great cities do best and turning that global reputation into jobs, opportunity and growth,” said UK prime minister Andy Burnham.

“This will be a world-class addition to Liverpool and another showcase for British music and creativity on the international stage.”

Past proposals to transform the Radio City Tower included the controversial plan to connect it to the roof of the 19th-century Central Library by a zipwire.

#67. First trophy, old tradition: St. Louis City SC brings U.S. Open Cup back to soccer's oldest American hotbed

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

It took over two hours of play and a penalty shootout for St. Louis City SC to win their first trophy in club history, defeating the Columbus Crew 5-3 on Wednesday in the Lamar Hunt U.S. Open Cup final. For the first time in 11 years, an Open Cup final was decided via a penalty shootout, and it came after St. Louis' starting keeper, Roman Burki, had to be taken to the hospital after being kneed in the head in the 78th minute of play.

Benjamin Lundt came in for Burki, making his fourth appearance of the season and his second in relief of Burki in an Open Cup penalty shootout. He only saved the first penalty by Santi Rodriguez, but that would be more than enough for St. Louis to get the job done. Their takers were clinical under pressure, as midfielder Daniel Edelman went down the middle to seal the win after playing all 120-plus minutes.

"It's just the next guy up. Roman was a hero. He had another save in the first half, and he's throwing his body on the line there. He's done it in Europe, and he's playing here now in MLS, and he's still doing what he did in Europe," Edelman said after the game to CBS Sports, praising his keepers following the match. "It's phenomenal for him and Ben. He started it for us in the Open Cup and had a big penalty save against Houston, and he did it here again today."

During the trophy lift, the team had Berki's jersey on display since he was at the hospital to be checked out. He was conscious and able to move while leaving the stadium, but it's never a good moment when a player has to be stretchered off, especially in a final, but his team managed to pull it off for him, adding to the Gateway to the West's rich history.

The city of St. Louis is no stranger to winning this competition, so it's only fitting that the first trophy in the history of the MLS club, which began play in 2023, is the U.S. Open Cup. Between 1920 and 1988, the city of St. Louis won the Open Cup 10 times, with the Busch Seniors being the most recent to win in 1988 before St. Louis City SC ended the drought. Edelman summed up what it meant to everyone.

"It's the first trophy for me and for a lot of guys in this city because you look up there and you see that [the support from away fans] and they completely deserve to have some silverware and for us as a team, what we've been through this year, it's phenomenal for us to have this night and have this trophy," he said.

Speaking to members of that '88 team, there were times that they didn't think that St. Louis would see another U.S. Open Cup title, but under Yoann Damet, this team has come together in a big way, and being able to overcome injuries to secure the Open Cup title with their backup keeper is another way that they've showcased their resilience. Heading into the weekend, St. Louis had a 21-game unbeaten run snapped, and there was no issue being able to regain focus in this critical moment.

Columbus dominated the chances for much of the game, taking 16 shots, but only three tested the St. Louis keepers as their defense bent but didn't break. This is preparation at its finest, but this team also isn't done. Damet wants to make sure to enjoy this trophy, but doing the double is the next goal as they've risen to second in the Western Conference. This is a team that will want to win the MLS Cup and secure the league title, and Damet made sure to acknowledge that.

"You always want to fill the trophy cabinet, and it takes time. Success is not built overnight, so we started the team thinking, let's implement a clear vision and a clear identity of how we want to do things, and see how far we can go in year one," Damet told CBS Sports. "This is still phase one of what we're gonna be, so I'm very happy with what we've been able to do. Our consistency in the league and winning a trophy is an amazing testament to the work of the guys. At the same time, it means so much for the city and for the community, and I'm so happy."

If this is only phase one with a 36-year-old coach at the helm who is also being backed by the front office, MLS may be hearing more from this club before long.

The big man, Guy Debord himself. The Society of the Spectacle, you say? Actually, I think we’re past that now. Hold the society bit. We’ll just take the spectacle.

The great Parisian Situationist philosopher wasn’t really one for managed fun. But it was impossible not to be bowled over by the extraordinary pre-match show for Paris Saint‑Germain versus Barcelona, Champions League Matchday Three.

The wild pyrotechnics, the plumes of smoke, the full-wrap light show, the lime-green robo‑text spelling out every player’s name on the pitch. Although not the Barcelona names, sadly, for anyone with an unrealised desire to see the words “ANTHONY GORDON” projected in vast letters for the delight of a shrieking crowd.

Admittedly, the Halloween‑themed tifo banner showing a masked Paris Saint-Germain serial killer-type literally butchering the Barcelona badge to death with his bloodied chainsaw was perhaps a bit much. As were the Barça Puta signs, which are now reportedly the subject of a complaint to Uefa.

But it was a wild and all‑consuming spectacle, annihilating every other impulse in your head to a green thought in a green shade. And in the process capturing the profound sense of cognitive dissonance in the city.

Just walking around the streets of central Paris earlier in the day generated its own mild whiplash. On the face of it this was still the same city of flâneurs and tourists and everyday bistro business, and not, repeat not, a civil war drama or some kind of emergent military caliphate.

But the sense of dread was also tangible. You still got the occasional crunch of glass underfoot. Here and there charred bikes and scooters had been politely piled to one side. Even close to the Parc, away from the main hotspots, shopkeepers could be seen emerging warily into their doorways at the sound of a police siren.

By Tuesday night, a total of 2,513 people had been taken into police custody after the events of the weekend. At least 420 rental bikes had been destroyed or maimed. Liberated high-value retail goods were already being sold on Vinted, a commendable show of entrepreneurial energy from the maligned younger generation.

Not that you’d suspect these two worlds had any kind of overlap, lost in the booming triumphalism around PSG’s 4–2 win, one of the most beautifully luminous football matches you’re ever likely to see. Or indeed reading the coverage of an event that seems to have been treated in France like its own clear, clean space, floating above the flames, all the chat about the brilliance of Monsieur Dynamite, Désiré Doué, the wonderful television audiences on Canal+ TV, the whole of European football “kneeling before the machine” (are we sure this isn’t to do with anarchic rejection of social control?). “Paris est insubmersible!” was the headline in Le Parisien. But is it?

Remember when football used to reflect the world? Or just provide clues, a commentary, some kind of metaphor? This is no longer the case. At a time when even the most thunderous of commentators is struggling to summarise all this in suitably trite and damning terms, it was hard to avoid the feeling Paris Saint-Germain, the pet power football project of the state of Qatar, expresses this dualism perfectly.

Sometimes you just have to let football tell you things. “People who want to cause trouble in the city centres next Saturday will be prevented,” Emmanuel Macron announced on Tuesday evening, speaking, as it happens, from Doha, where he met Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad al-Thani, ultimate owner of PSG.

Macron is said to be considering deploying the French military to keep the peace on the streets before the next planned protests, although even here he may have to ask Qatar’s permission if he wants to deploy any of the French troops currently deployed working its missile systems, or embedded in the Doha security services.

The key event Macron is tooling up for is this Saturday night, 24 October, the date the gilets jaunes social media feed has set for an even greater swell of disorder. And again this will be football adjacent, with PSG playing Lyon at home on Sunday, a top‑of‑the-table capital‑city clash played out against whatever wreckage is left, however many thousands of Dot bikes are savaged, whatever guerrilla skirmishes are still playing out in the Marais.

What does this really have to do with football and the Ici C’est Paris brand appropriated by a state outreach project? Well, what are these demonstrations anyway? There have been many theories put forward as to what exactly is happening here, what is the spark, the kindling, the fire behind this disorder. School protests were the original line. Others have now roped in some kind of nebulous omni-cause, Instagram-activism, perhaps even a barbarian takeover, the decline of the west, savagery at the gates, of the type Donald Trump pretends to see.

Do you need a single reason? Take a look around. Modern life is pretty alarming on a minute‑to‑minute basis. Disaffection, alienation, a sense of being transformed in real time into a passive economic consumer unit, a set of eyeballs, an avatar of consumption. French youth unemployment is close to 20%. Robots nobody wants are being lined up to take jobs that don’t exist. Why don’t these young people want to join a youth club? Why don’t they sign up for the Liberal Democrats? Why are they enjoying empowered disorder? Maybe because nothing is yours around here, not even the things thrust into your brain as digital distractions. So why not grab something when you can? Debord would have called this “a vicious circle of isolation”, lonely crowd, technologies based on isolation.

On the other hand, what is PSG in Paris, other than the perfect expression of this? It often seems to surprise people that celebrations of PSG victories end up with riots and burnt-out cars. But Paris is both a beautiful and deeply dysfunctional city, a place of make‑believe and deprivation, where the centre is a perfect, performative relic, the suburbs shunted out to the ring roads, a life lived inside a box, noses pressed up against the glass, peering through your binoculars at the city of someone else’s love.

Paris is the divide between overclass and churn expressed in a single city. It’s everything you might have had but never will. And PSG represent this like no other entity, a football club owned as an act of outreach by a staggeringly rich inherited monarchy, weaponised passion, borrowed culture, engineered spectacle, deeply cynical but also deeply seductive.

Why are the Qataris splurging hundreds of millions into this project? Because they just really like high-pressing positional football? Qatar is the indentured labour state that employs Colin Kaepernick to promote its outreach projects. It will hymn its connections to the footballers of the banlieue, which is true and real and valuable, but not because of anything this political project wants or needs other than public relations.

It is in its own way a perfect human lab experiment, synthesised beauty, collectivism, with a cold, cold hand at its heart. And this is why there weren’t two versions of Paris in evidence on Tuesday night, just the same one, the dark side and the shiny side, an engine of the same divisions, the same kind of violence, right down to the sight of kids in PSG training gear dodging the police or taunting the barricades at the Place de la République.

And now we have Sunday night to come, more Ici C’est Paris, more PASSION. What kind of background will it be played out against? Does it matter under the lights? Will anyone notice?

Le Monde has described these events as a very basic test of the republic, its ability to bend, to remain essentially itself, the state’s willingness to pit itself against its own people. Football, as ever, holds these answers already. PSG’s Qatari owners have spent the past 15 years flaunting this city like a global luxury brand, extending its reach, creating its own ideal of conspicuous consumption, spectacle, triumph, son et lumière, bread and roses. As ever, if you want to know how and why, football has the answers.
