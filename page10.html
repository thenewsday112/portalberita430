#152. Gianluigi Donnarumma: ‘Mistakes’ analysed by Enzo Maresca after Man City goalkeeper receives criticism

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Oliver Thomas | 27 Oct 2026 13:35

Manchester City manager Enzo Maresca has defended Gianluigi Donnarumma following a recent dip in form, insisting that mistakes are an inevitable consequence of football’s relentless fixture schedule.

Donnarumma has established himself as Man City’s undisputed first-choice shot-stopper since arriving at the Etihad Stadium from Paris Saint-Germain last summer for a reported £26m.

The Italy international has delivered several important performances between the sticks for the Citizens, but he has also been prone to making costly mistakes leading to goals conceded.

Donnarumma was at fault for the second goal conceded by City in their 2-1 Premier League defeat at Aston Villa last weekend, allowing a low shot from Nicolas Jackson to fly under his 6ft 5in frame standing in the centre of the goal.

The 27-year-old should also have done better with PSG’s first and fourth goals scored against City in their 4-2 Champions League defeat earlier this month, while his distribution and reactions when dealing with corners have also led to criticism.

Donnarumma is one of five Man City stars who will miss Wednesday’s EFL Cup fourth-round tie against Brighton & Hove Albion after being allowed time off following the loss at Villa Park.

Number two Geronimo Rulli will come in against Brighton and start just his second game for the club since joining in the summer, but Donnarumma is expected to return for Saturday’s Premier League clash against the same opponent.

Speaking at a press conference on Tuesday morning, Maresca was asked to share his thoughts on Donnarumma’s dip in form and whether it is a concern for him going forward.

He told reporters: "When a football player or manager don't get results or make some mistakes, it's normal that it [criticism] is a consequence.

“Gigio, I think, since we started has done well. It's part of the season, they're playing so many games, so many minutes that sometimes it's almost impossible to not make a mistake.

"He's quite a strong guy. He knows anyway that when he makes some mistake, he doesn't need us to tell him that was a mistake. He recognises that and it's part of the game."

Marc Guehi is another Man City player who has come under fire in recent weeks, making his latest mistake for the opening goal conceded in the defeat at Villa Park.

However, Maresca is pleased with Guehi's reaction and has been impressed with the defender’s ability to shake off last weekend’s disappointment.

“I’m completely, especially in this moment, happy with Marc,” said Maresca. “The way he reacted was fantastic. He is a top guy, a leader, one of the captains and I love Marc to be honest. When they make some mistakes, they recognise. Marc is aware now.

“He’s played all minutes since the start of the season with us and the international team. In the last two weeks he didn’t play Champions League game, he played 45 minutes the other day, so he’s completely okay.”

Unlike Donnarumma, Guehi is available for selection against Brighton on Wednesday and is one of three senior centre-backs, along with Abdukodir Khusanov and Vitor Reis, in contention to start.

#158. Man City vs. Brighton team news: Erling Haaland among five players to miss EFL Cup tie as Enzo Maresca issues update

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Oliver Thomas | 27 Oct 2026 11:18, Last updated: 27 Oct 2026 11:49

Erling Haaland will miss Manchester City’s EFL Cup fourth-round tie at home against Brighton & Hove Albion on Wednesday night, manager Enzo Maresca has confirmed.

The prolific Norwegian recovered from a knock to his foot to score in City’s 2-1 Premier League defeat at Aston Villa last weekend – his 13th goal in 11 appearances across all competitions.

Haaland, who finished eighth in the 2026 Ballon d’Or rankings, also has an impressive scoring record against Brighton, netting seven times in his last seven encounters with the Seagulls.

However, Man City’s No.9 is one of five first-team stars who will not be involved on Wednesday along with Gianluigi Donnarumma, Elliot Anderson, Josko Gvardiol and captain Ruben Dias.

Speaking at a press conference on Tuesday morning, Maresca allayed injury fears over the Citizens quintet and confirmed that the decision has been made to give them an additional period of rest.

"After the Villa game (last Saturday), we gave five players three days off," Maresca, told reporters. "Erling, Ruben, Elliot, Gigi and Josko. They are all on holiday. Three days off, energy, go, come back full of energy and then go again.”

This decision comes after Maresca told reporters earlier this month that he needs to rest some of his first-team stars during the club’s busy fixture schedule, particularly those who have also played regularly for their countries at the World Cup and during the recent international break.

In the absence of first-choice goalkeeper Donnarumma, Maresca has also confirmed that summer signing Geronimo Rulli will start between the sticks for his second appearance for the club.

Rulli kept a clean sheet on his Man City debut in the 5-0 home victory over Norwich City in the EFL Cup third round last month.

It was also a memorable night for 17-year-old academy graduate Floyd Samba, who scored a brace on his full Man City debut, and he could be handed another start up front in Haaland’s absence against Brighton.

Marc Guehi, Vitor Reis and Abdukodir Khusanov are all contenders to start at centre-back in the absence of Dias, while Nico O'Reilly and Rayan Ait-Nouri are set to battle for a start at left-back now that Gvardiol is being given a rest.

In the absence of Anderson, fellow summer signing Ayyoub Bouaddi could be recalled in centre-midfield, with Enzo Fernandez and Mateo Kovacic also contenders to start in the middle of the pitch.

Man City are preparing to face Brighton twice within the space of just four days, with their EFL Cup tie followed by another home encounter in the Premier League on Saturday - first versus third in the table.

"First of all, [Brighton] are doing very well; both games are going to be tough games,” said Maresca. “In terms of how we plan the two games, they are so close so we need to make some changes.

“But again, we have one more session this afternoon and then we'll decide (on a starting lineup) for tomorrow and Saturday.

"They are very brave in terms of aggression and when they have the ball, they attack with so many players and are creating many chances, scoring many goals, so we need to pay attention defensively and we need to do the right things offensively."

Brighton have drawn widespread praise throughout season both for their free-scoring displays and statement victories in the Premier League, including a 3-0 home win over champions Arsenal last month.

The Seagulls also beat Man United 3-2 in the EFL Cup third round, but they are seeking to bounce back from a Premier League defeat last weekend, losing 2-0 at Liverpool, while they have never beaten the Citizens away from home in 17 previous attempts (D3 L14).

#396. OHLAB experiments with colour-blocking and cork in old and new buildings of Terreno Barrio Hotel in Mallorca

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Photography by Jose Hevia

Mallorca-based architecture and interiors studio OHLAB has overseen the design of a hotel in Palma, including a renovated heritage building from 1935 and a new structure built from cross-laminated timber and cork.

The 41-room Terreno Barrio Hotel was envisioned as “a catalyst for the neighbourhood” of El Terreno, in the west of the Mallorcan city, serving as a community hub as well as a destination for tourists.

“Terreno is not conceived as a tourist object imposed on its context, but as a device for exchange – a hotel that, before being a hotel, aims to be an extension of the barrio,” said OHLAB founders Paloma Hernaiz and Jaime Oliver.

“This is not a slogan; it is the principle that orders programme, thresholds, atmospheres and materiality.”

The hotel’s reception is in the renovated 1935 building, a five-storey apartment block designed by local architect Francesc Casas, along with the restaurant and bar, a club, and a roof bar.

OHLAB refers to this building as “the stage”, the eye-catching focal point of the complex, while the new structure is described as “the bazaar”, its civic heart.

Wrapping a new public plaza, this second block houses the community-led spaces, including a cafe, coworking areas, a bookshop and gallery, pop-up retail, a gym, a spa and a cinema.

OHLAB adopted different strategies for the architecture and interior design of the two structures, creatively exploring the themes of building and material reuse in various ways.

“Here, circularity was understood not only as an environmental or material strategy, but also as an economic, social and territorial one, a way of keeping value within the local ecosystem by activating residents, culture, crafts, suppliers and everyday life,” they said.

In the renovated building, interior design revives the existing structure rather than covering it.

Communal spaces have a theatrical character, with patterned floor tiles and heavy curtains. Highlights include a golden mirror bar and a wool curtain installation by artist Sylvia Sanchez Montoya.

In the bedrooms, colourblocking creates a horizontal divide between top and bottom.

Floors and furniture feature matching terrazzo, produced by local tile manufacturer Huguet using the project’s demolition waste.

Materials are even more prominent in the seven-storey new building, thanks to the choice of materials. Cork clads the courtyard-facing north facade, while the other exterior walls feature timber louvres that shade the windows from direct sunlight.

Inside, the exposed CLT structure is brought to life through more playful use of colour and materials. The cafe and shop is a key example, with shades of brown and lime green extending across to the terrazzo floors and wooden furniture.

The bedrooms in this block have a different character, with murals by Mallorcan artist Albert Pinya, bathrooms housed inside mirrored boxes, and timber flooring that merges with joinery.

OHLAB was appointed by José Taltavull, who developed the concept for the hotel after being brought on board by hotelier Lydia Piñero.

Taltavull appointed graphic design office StudioRoses to oversee the hotel’s visual identity, which fed into OHLAB’s scheme.

Various elements were picked out in the same red as the hotel logo, including the ramped floor between the bar and reception, the stage in the club, and the sculptural stools located in the bar.

Most of the furniture was either designed by OHLAB or sourced from Mallorca-based manufacturers. A DJ table was made from cork leftover from the construction, while Mallorcan artist Pedro Óliver contributed the neon lighting pieces that feature throughout.

Terreno Barrio Hotel is the latest in a series of major openings in Palma in recent years, with the city in the midst of a cultural renaissance.

El Terreno is a major part of that story, particularly since Mallorca-based shoe brand Camper completed its 15,000-square-metre Project Gomila in 2023, which includes the new Jasper Morrison-designed Can Pa bakery.

OHLAB’s other projects in the city include the Can Santacilia residential development and the Paral·lel apartment complex.
